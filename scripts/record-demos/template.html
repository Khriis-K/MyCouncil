Bring a hard decision. Seat a council to argue it out.

MyCouncil turns a personal dilemma into a debate between 3 to 7 AI counselors, each with a distinct cognitive style. You see where they disagree, put your own view to them, and correspond one-on-one with any of them. Counselors remember what you told them in earlier sessions, through a retrieval pipeline I benchmarked on a held-out test set.

Points of contention

The dossier

Correspond with a counselor

Click any seat for a first impression, then read the full opinion: their read on your situation and the steps they'd take. A correspondence column beside it takes your follow-up questions.

The record

Add to the record

New details surface as you reflect. Add them, and the council reconvenes and updates its advice around what changed. The record keeps every amendment.

The matter

Your temperament

Pick your MBTI type to decide who sits on your council and how they speak to you. A few statements check the fit before you confirm.

Or keep the balanced panel, and choose a reflection focus: Decision-Making, Emotional Processing, or Creative Problem Solving.

Long-term memory, measured

server/memory · bench/memory

Counselors recall what you said in past sessions. The interesting part is less the pipeline than how it was evaluated: a reproducible offline benchmark with separate dev and test splits, where the selector design was frozen on dev before the test set was touched.

  • Off-the-shelf rerankers hurt.Both cross-encoders lowered recall versus plain dense retrieval, especially on implicit probes, so they are off by default.
  • The LLM selector helps most where it's hardest.On implicit probes, which share almost no words with the answer, recall@5 rises from 0.52 to roughly 0.80–0.86.
  • Stage 1 isn't the bottleneck.Pool recall@30 is 0.92–1.00 across slices, so the gains come from ordering candidates.
  • BM25 and hybrid didn't beat dense on dev (0.606 and 0.762 vs 0.775), so dense stays the default.
Limitations, stated up front. The dataset is LLM-generated. A human review of a 20-probe sample found 6 defects (30%, 95% CI 14.5–51.9%), which were fixed by hand along with 19 superseded-gold references; the rest were not individually reviewed. With 160 test probes, gaps of a few points are within noise, so read the relative comparisons as the signal.
