Bring a hard decision. Summon a council to argue it out.

MyCouncil turns a personal dilemma into a debate between 3 to 7 AI counselors, each with a distinct cognitive style. You watch where they disagree, push back with your own thoughts, and chat one-on-one with any of them. Counselors remember what you told them in earlier sessions, through a retrieval pipeline I benchmarked on a held-out test set.

What it does

Recorded in the running app

Council clash

Debate mode draws tension lines between counselors who disagree. Open one and the pair argues it out live.

  • Inject your own comment and both sides respond to it
  • The decision matrix updates as the debate moves, adding new criteria

1-on-1 with a counselor

Click any counselor for their read on your situation, a step-by-step action protocol, and a direct chat line for follow-up questions.

Iterative refinement

New details surface as you reflect. Add them, and the council reconvenes and updates its advice around what changed.

Your cognitive style

Pick your MBTI type to tune how the council speaks to you. A short validation quiz checks the fit before you commit.

Or keep the default balanced panel, and choose a reflection lens: Decision-Making, Emotional Processing, or Creative Problem Solving.

Long-term memory, measured

server/memory · bench/memory

Counselors recall what you said in past sessions. The interesting part is less the pipeline than how it was evaluated: a reproducible offline benchmark with separate dev and test splits, where the selector design was frozen on dev before the test set was touched.

  • Off-the-shelf rerankers hurt.Both cross-encoders lowered recall versus plain dense retrieval, especially on implicit probes, so they are off by default.
  • The LLM selector helps most where it's hardest.On implicit probes, which share almost no words with the answer, recall@5 rises from 0.52 to roughly 0.80–0.86.
  • Stage 1 isn't the bottleneck.Pool recall@30 is 0.92–1.00 across slices, so the gains come from ordering candidates.
  • BM25 and hybrid didn't beat dense on dev (0.606 and 0.762 vs 0.775), so dense stays the default.
Limitations, stated up front. The dataset is LLM-generated. A human review of a 20-probe sample found 6 defects (30%, 95% CI 14.5–51.9%), which were fixed by hand along with 19 superseded-gold references; the rest were not individually reviewed. With 160 test probes, gaps of a few points are within noise, so read the relative comparisons as the signal.
